# Satiate GLP-1 Tracker — brief

**Use case.** Satiate is a phone app for people on weekly GLP-1 shots (semaglutide, tirzepatide). In one place it logs the injection (dose, site, pen), tracks side effects (nausea, fatigue, appetite) and nudges the habits that make the medication work: protein, water, steps. Users are adults 30–60, often new to injecting and anxious about week-one symptoms; clinicians and creators recommend the app. The team needs in-app icons, a landing-page hero, feature cards, an App Store / social banner, a clinician lower third for their YouTube explainers, a launch story and a hero illustration.

**Idea.** *A calm little gadget for a medical routine.* Early-2000s consumer tech made health feel friendly: brushed-silver MP3 players, aqua jelly buttons, translucent cases. Satiate borrows that: chrome capsules, glossy pill-shaped buttons and a silver weekly dial, so a weekly shot feels like a ritual with a nice object, not a chore.

**Reference.** Y2K product design (the iMac G3 / iPod era, Sony's silver Walkman ads, Tamagotchi packaging) set with art-school type, like a Wallpaper* magazine cover from 2001.

**Palette "Chrome Y2K" (light).** `#DADDE3` aluminium (bg) · `#C3C8D1` brushed steel (surface, chrome gradients) · `#101219` ink (all small text, outlines) · `#3D5AFE` electric blue (accent: the dial's active day, CTAs, glowing LEDs) · `#B8C0CC` silver haze (accent2: grids, secondary rings) · `#FF3DB4` jelly pink (pop: one dot, the "logged" tick, a spark). Variants borrow Lilac Haze, Sunset Sorbet, Nordic Frost, Neo-brutalist and Ink & Gold.

**Type "Art school".** Syne 800 for hero words, set very wide and tight. Bricolage Grotesque for copy. Major Mono Display for labels, dates, doses and eyebrows (lowercase, wide-tracked).

**Shared motif: the weekly dose dial.** A chrome ring split into 7 day ticks (M T W T F S S) with one blue glowing segment for shot day and a pink dot for "logged". It appears as the banner hero, the launch video's clock, the lower third's badge, the scene's phone screen and the 3D pen's dose window. Chrome capsules (pill-shaped glossy bars) carry the labels.
